A little about me

Self-taught,
and still building.

I spent three years studying Electronics Engineering at TUP Taguig before leaving to work as a line technician, maintaining test handlers for semiconductor chips at Analog Devices. Somewhere in that year of troubleshooting machines, I got curious about the software running them and started teaching myself to code.

Even growing up, I've always been a bit lazy, in the sense that I hate doing the same thing twice. Instead of fighting that instinct, I turned it into an advantage: if a task is repetitive, I'd rather spend the time to automate it once than do it by hand forever. That mindset is what pulled me into the world of automation, and it still drives most of what I build today.

My interests are broad on purpose. I believe everything is interconnected, and that you learn faster by learning indirectly, the same way learning Spanish makes Italian and Portuguese far easier to pick up. Every new field I explore, whether it's software, electronics, or data, sharpens how I approach the others. I'm not tied to a single title, and I'm open to remote, hybrid, or on-site roles across technician, PLC programming, data analysis, cybersecurity, and full-stack development.

Always learning. Always building.
